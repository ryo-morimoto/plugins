# Axes: purpose and mode

Pick one option per axis on purpose. Running without choosing defaults to "attack one proposition with your own judgment", which is only one mode among many.

## 1. Purpose
- **Destructive**: argue against a claim. Subtypes in axis 2.
- **Constructive**: support or build a claim. Subtypes in axis 3.
- **Both at once**: one scenario refutes the old view and yields the new one.
- **Heuristic**: generate ideas; the result is investigated afterwards, not trusted.
- **Illustrative**: make a claim clear. Gives no reason for or against it.
- **Rhetorical**: persuade. Never present this as evidence.

## 2. Destructive subtypes
- Expose a contradiction inside the claim.
- Show conflict with other firmly held beliefs.
- Undermine a hidden assumption.
- Counter thought experiment: same setup, opposite verdict.
- "Called impossible, yet possible" versus "called possible, yet impossible".
- Counterexample to sufficiency (a factor the claim failed to exclude) versus to necessity (a required condition is irrelevant). Both hint at a repair.

## 3. Constructive subtypes
- Mediative: make an existing theory easier to grasp or apply.
- Conjectural: establish a phenomenon, then guess an explanation.
- Direct: go from the imagined phenomenon to a theory.
- Possibility-showing: one case proves something can happen.
- Analogical support: non-conclusive; needs a principle covering both cases.

## 4. Question type
- What would happen? (factual)
- How should we describe it? (conceptual)
- How should we evaluate it? (normative)

## 5. Open or closed
- Closed: test a view you already hold.
- Open: you do not know the answer; vary a known scenario and see.

## 6. Time direction
- Before the event: what could have led here?
- After the event: what follows?
- Counterfactual history: change one cause, keep the rewrite of prior conditions minimal, stay consistent.
