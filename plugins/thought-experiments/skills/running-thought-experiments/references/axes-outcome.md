# Axes: handling and attacking results

## 10. What to do with a result
A surprising verdict does not say what to change. List the options, then choose with the user.

- **Give up the weakest belief**: rank the beliefs in play by how firmly they are held; drop the lowest.
- **Responses to a counterexample**:
  - Surrender: drop the claim.
  - Bar it: argue the case is not a real instance.
  - Restrict: narrow the claim to exclude it.
  - Reinterpret: show the case fits after all.
  - Incorporate: find the hidden assumption the case violates and add it as a condition.
- **Which premise to reject** in a refutation:
  - the claim itself;
  - that the claim implies the consequence;
  - that the consequence would hold in the scenario;
  - that the consequence is absurd;
  - that the scenario is possible.
- **Reflective balance**: revise the verdict, the principle, or both until they fit.

## 11. Attacks to run on your own result
Where to strike:
- Scenario: incoherent, or underdescribed so the verdict is not fixed.
- Run-through: verdict reached by bias or by a detail of the story.
- Reasoning: an unjustified jump; two concepts treated as one.
- Analogy: relevant differences from the real case.
- Target: conclusion misses what is at issue.

Critic's moves:
- Apply a different theory to the same scenario.
- Refuse the idealisation.
- Show the claim was misapplied.
- Offer another resolution.

Misleading-by-design checks:
- Wrong problem substituted for the real one.
- Forced choice that hides a third answer.
- Premises smuggled in by implication.

Structural checks:
- **Tractability**: the setup must not make the conclusion true by construction.
- **Validity**: each step follows.
- **Soundness**: premises fairly represent the thing discussed.
- **Pair test**: if an equally good scenario gives the opposite verdict, at least one fails.
- **Presentation effects**: wording, order, names and emotional load can move the verdict.
